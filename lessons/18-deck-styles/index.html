Deck Styles

Aggro, control, combo and stun: what each one wants, and how to spot it. 4 min · 6 flashcards

Four ways to win

Every deck wants to bring your Life Points to 0. They just take different roads to get there. Most decks lean on one of four styles.

StyleExample decksDescription
AggroBlackwing, Diva Hero BeatPuts strong attackers on the field early and hits you every turn.
ControlGladiator Beast, GeminiRuns lots of removal and Traps. Trades card for card and wins late.
ComboQuickdraw Plant, Junk Frog, LightswornChains card effects together to build a big board in one turn.
StunHero Beat, Anti-MetaLocks the game with cards that stop both players from doing something.

Real decks mix styles. Blackwing is mostly aggro, but it also runs 13 Traps. Hero Beat attacks with plain beaters, but its Traps lock the game. Use the style as a first guess, not a final answer.

Think of it like this

Aggro is a sprinter. Control is a marathon runner. Combo builds a machine and then flips the switch. Stun locks the door and fights you in the hallway.

How each style looks in a deck list

You can often guess a style just by counting card types in the deck lists.

DeckMonstersSpellsTrapsDescription

Junk Frog25123Lots of monsters, few Traps. Its combo works best with an empty back row.
Hero Beat121117Few monsters, many Traps, including 2 Royal Oppression and 2 Skill Drain.

How to tell what you face

You rarely see a deck list in a real game. Watch what your opponent does in the first two turns.

What you seeLikely styleDescription
A 1700+ attacker and 1 or 2 set cardsAggroThey want to start dealing damage right away.
Many Special Summons in one turnComboCards like and tokens turn into Synchros fast.
Three or four set cards, one monsterControlThey plan to answer each of your plays one at a time.
A face-up lock cardStun, or on the field.
Cards sent from the Deck to the GYComboLightsworn and Vayu Turbo fill their Graveyard on purpose.
in the GY, no back rowComboFrog Monarchs. Treeborn only comes back if they control no Spells or Traps.

What beats each style

Once you know the style, you know which of your cards matter most.

  • Against aggro: Traps that stop attacks, like and Mirror Force. Monsters that stop a direct attack, like Battle Fader. Make every attack cost them a card.
  • Against control: Do not dump your hand onto the field. Play one threat at a time and keep the rest. Spells that clear Traps, like Heavy Storm, matter more.
  • Against combo: Stop the first step. A combo needs every link. negates an effect that starts when a monster is Normal Summoned. Royal Oppression negates Special Summons. Solemn Judgment can stop the Synchro at the end.
  • Against stun: Remove the lock before you commit. Mystical Space Typhoon and Dust Tornado are your keys. Until then, play cards the lock does not touch.
The big idea

Ask two questions every game: "What does my opponent need to happen?" and "Which of my cards stops that?" Save that card for the right moment.

The Side Deck follows the styles

Side Decks are built to fight styles. Look at the Blackwing list. Its Side Deck holds 2 Royal Oppression, 2 Fossil Dyna Pachycephalo and 2 Pulling the Rug. All of them hurt combo decks that Special Summon a lot or use Normal Summon effects.

The next six lessons look at real decks one by one. For each, you will learn its engine, its plan, and how to beat it. The Library has side decking guides by E3 Yu-Gi-Oh and IMNerd (Frazier Smith) if you want more.

Common mistakes
  • Siding cards that beat a style your opponent is not playing.
  • Playing the same way against every deck. Your plan changes with theirs.
  • Guessing a style from one card. Wait for two or three clues.

Recap

  • Aggro hits early, control wins late, combo builds a big turn, stun locks the game.
  • Most decks mix two styles.
  • Watch the first two turns: set cards, Special Summons, lock cards and the Graveyard.
  • Pick your answers by what the opponent needs to happen.
