Choosing Your Deck

Pick one deck, then learn it until you know every card. 3 min · 5 flashcards

Tier is not everything

A Best deck you don't understand loses to a Rogue deck you know by heart. Pick a strong deck, but pick one you want to play many times.

Pick a deck that fits you

Lesson 18 sorted decks into styles: aggro, control, combo and stun. Ask yourself a few questions.

  • Do you like to win by battle? Look at decks built around attacking, like Gladiator Beasts (Lesson 25).
  • Do you like long turns with many steps? Look at decks with a big engine, like Vayu Turbo or Dragon Turbo.
  • Do you like to answer what your opponent does? Look at control decks with many traps.
  • Do you like to stop the other deck from playing? Look at stun decks with cards like Royal Oppression.

Learn it well: a plan

  1. Read the list. Open it from All Decks. Tap each card you don't know and read its Edison text and rulings.
  2. Draw test hands. Press "Test hand" on the deck page. Ask: what is my best first turn with these 6 cards?
  3. Copy it to the builder. Press "Open in Deck Builder" to make your own copy in the Deck Builder. Change it only after you know why each card is there.
  4. Practice the combos. Load the deck on the practice board and play out your best turns by hand.
  5. Play games. Play people as much as you can: friends, online, or at locals.
  6. Watch the experts. The Library has deck guides for Blackwings, Quickdraw, Dragons, Diva Hero, Hero Beat, Vayu Turbo and Frogs.
  7. Learn your side. Know which Side Deck cards come in against each top deck (Lesson 29).
Common mistake

Switching decks after every loss. Each switch starts your learning over. Stay with one deck long enough to know why you lost.

Recap

  • The site has 55 lists in five tiers, from Best to Casual.
  • Pick a strong deck in a style you enjoy.
  • Learn it with test hands, the builder, the board and duels.
  • Stay with one deck long enough to really know it.
